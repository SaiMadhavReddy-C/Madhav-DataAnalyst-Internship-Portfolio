Statistical Validation

Hypothesis Testing

Testing whether average transaction sales differed between female and male transactions.

Business Question

Is there statistical evidence that the mean transaction sales for female and male transactions are different?

Null Hypothesis H₀
Female and male mean transaction sales are equal.
Alternative H₁
Female and male mean transaction sales are different.
Statistical Test
Welch's t-test
Significance Level
α = 0.05
P-value
0.495011
95% Confidence Interval
[-₹19,079.85, ₹9,231.58]
Fail to Reject H₀ at α = 0.05
The p-value is greater than the 0.05 significance level, so the analysis does not provide sufficient statistical evidence to conclude that the two group means differ. This is a statistical finding for this dataset and should not be interpreted as proof that gender has no effect on purchasing behavior.
Portfolio Highlights

From Data to Decision Support

The project follows a complete analytics workflow.

01 · Trust the Data
Profiled missing values, repeated identifiers, formatting consistency and revenue calculations before using the dataset for analysis.
02 · Ask Business Questions
Used SQL and exploratory analysis to examine sales performance across time, products, categories, cities, gender and age groups.
03 · Build KPIs
Defined core measures including total sales, average transaction value, transaction count, unique customers and units sold.
04 · Communicate Visually
Converted the analysis into an interactive dashboard designed to make category, product and sales performance easier to explore.
05 · Validate Statistically
Used a Welch independent two-sample t-test and confidence interval to validate a business question rather than relying only on descriptive differences.
06 · Document the Journey
Organized the work into a public GitHub portfolio containing cleaning scripts, reports, SQL, datasets, dashboard assets and the final presentation.
